# Data-Driven Resource Allocation: Power BI Dashboards for Budget & Utilization

**Problem Statement**: A design team of 30 designers juggling 50+ concurrent projects had no clear visibility into capacity, utilization, or budget — forcing 5 design leads to make resource decisions based on intuition and manual Smartsheets data that wasn't structured for management use.

---

## Situation

Electrolux Group's Wellbeing design area operates at significant scale: 30 designers, 50+ ongoing projects at any given time, and 5 design leads who each manage a team. Designers reported their time in Smartsheets, but the data wasn't presented in a way that was useful for managers. There was no centralized view of how resources were being allocated, whether the team was over- or under-capacity, or how budgets were tracking against plans.

The information existed — but it was locked in raw data that nobody could act on.

## Challenge

The challenges were specific and compounding:

- **No capacity visibility**: No way to see when project demands would exceed team capacity
- **Intake uncertainty**: No clarity on how many projects the team could realistically take on at any given time
- **Blind resource allocation**: Leads couldn't see resource allocation in advance or anticipate overbookings
- **Manual reporting waste**: Time spent on manual reporting that produced reports too general to be useful
- **Missing detail**: Reports lacked specifics on how people and budgets were actually being used
- **No KPIs**: No clear metrics to measure team utilization or budget performance

Design leads were flying blind, and the operational cost of that blindness was growing with every new project added to the queue.

## Approach

I used a triple diamond design thinking framework — Understand, Create, Develop — to tackle this as a structured initiative rather than a quick fix.

### Understand
- Discussed with managers across the area to identify their specific reporting needs and pain points
- Performed benchmarking with other internal departments and external companies to analyze how they leverage reporting data — including chart types, visual formats, and tools
- Collaborated with DesignOps peers from other Electrolux product lines to compare challenges and share solutions for improving data visibility and reporting consistency

### Create
- After benchmarking, determined that Power BI was the right tool for our needs
- Designed the dashboard layout and visual hierarchy to make complex information feel simple and actionable
- Self-taught Power BI through online tutorials and hands-on experimentation — and discovered how LLMs could accelerate the development process
- After several weeks of trial and iteration, built a functional, data-driven tool that clearly visualizes the team's capacity, utilization, and dependencies across projects

### Develop
- Presented the first version to area managers, who found it immediately useful for visualizing workload distribution and identifying bottlenecks
- Incorporated their feedback on specific metrics and visual details into subsequent iterations
- Evolved the dashboards through multiple rounds of refinement based on real usage

## My Role

I owned this initiative end to end — from stakeholder research through tool selection, self-learning, design, development, and iteration. I was the architect of the data model, the designer of the visual layout, and the person who drove adoption by embedding the dashboards into how the team actually works. This also required me to learn an entirely new tool (Power BI) and find ways to accelerate my own learning using AI tools.

## Impact

- Dashboards are now actively used in planning and decision-making meetings to allocate design resources across 50+ projects
- Became the key reference point for understanding capacity, balancing workloads, and anticipating debt to other areas
- Enabled design leads to make proactive, data-driven decisions instead of reactive, intuition-based ones
- Replaced manual Smartsheets reporting with automated, interactive visualizations — saving significant time and improving accuracy
- Established a data-driven operational culture within the design area, where resource and budget discussions are grounded in real numbers

---

**Key Takeaway**: The most impactful operational tools aren't always the most sophisticated — they're the ones that answer the right questions at the right time. Building this system required more curiosity than expertise: understanding what managers actually needed, benchmarking how others solved similar problems, and being willing to learn a new tool from scratch. What began as an exploratory project became the operational backbone for a 30-person design team.
