## Design Direction

### Visual Style
- **Clean and structured** — the portfolio itself should demonstrate operational thinking
- **Minimal color palette**: 2-3 colors max, professional but warm
- **Typography**: Modern sans-serif (Inter, DM Sans, or similar)
- **Layout**: Generous whitespace, clear hierarchy, scannable
- **Diagrams over decoration**: Use process diagrams, timelines, and frameworks as visuals — not stock photos

### Why This Style Works for DesignOps
DesignOps portfolios are different from design portfolios. Hiring managers aren't looking for pixel-perfect UI work — they're looking for evidence of operational thinking, structured problem-solving, and the ability to communicate complex processes clearly. The portfolio's own structure and clarity IS the portfolio proof.

## Platform Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Notion** (public page) | Fast to build, easy to update, supports all content types | Limited visual customization, "Notion" branding |
| **Framer** | Beautiful templates, custom domain, fast | Some learning curve, monthly cost |
| **Squarespace** | Polished templates, reliable | Less flexible for custom layouts |
| **ReadMe / GitBook** | Great for structured content, clean | More "docs" feel, less portfolio-like |
| **Simple HTML/CSS** | Full control, fast loading, free hosting (GitHub Pages) | Requires coding or template |
| **PDF Portfolio** | Universal, works offline, easy to send | Static, can't update dynamically |

**Recommendation**: Start with a well-structured PDF portfolio (immediate need for job applications) and build a simple web version in Framer or as a static site for ongoing use.
