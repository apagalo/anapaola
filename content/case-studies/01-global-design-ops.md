# Case Study 01 — Scaling Design Operations Across a Global Organization

---

## Hero

**Problem statement:**
Electrolux Group's Wellbeing and Small Domestic Appliances (SDA) design teams — 30 designers managing 50+ concurrent projects across 5 design leads — operated with no centralized operational structure. Projects lacked visibility, workflows varied by team, and cross-functional coordination relied on informal channels — leading to misaligned priorities, duplicated efforts, and inconsistent delivery timelines.

---

## TL;DR

**Problem:** 30 designers, 50+ projects, zero operational structure. Coordination was scattered across emails and ad hoc meetings.

**What I did:** Built the DesignOps function from scratch over 3 years — starting with centralized project tracking, then standardizing workflows, then scaling into strategic operations.

**Impact:**
- Single operational backbone for the entire design org
- Reduced coordination overhead for 5 design leads
- Full portfolio visibility for leadership decision-making
- DesignOps maturity from ad hoc to strategic function

---

## Situation

Electrolux Group is a global leader in home appliances, with design teams distributed across Europe, Asia, and Latin America. The Wellbeing and SDA portfolios span product categories from air purifiers to kitchen appliances, each with distinct design requirements, stakeholder groups, and go-to-market timelines.

When I joined as Design Projects Coordinator in 2022, the design organization had talented teams producing strong work — but the operational infrastructure connecting them was fragmented. With 30 designers across 5 teams handling 50+ projects simultaneously, there was no unified system for tracking work across portfolios, no standardized workflows, and limited visibility into how resources were being allocated. Designers reported time in Smartsheets, but the data wasn't structured for management use.

---

## Challenge

The core challenge was threefold. First, project coordination happened through scattered channels — email threads, ad hoc meetings, and individual trackers — making it nearly impossible to get a clear picture of what was happening across the full design portfolio. Second, each team had developed their own ways of working, which created friction whenever cross-functional collaboration was needed. Third, as the design organization grew, the lack of operational structure became a bottleneck. Senior designers and design leads spent significant time on coordination work rather than the creative and strategic work they were hired to do.

The question was: how do you build an operational backbone for a global design team without slowing them down or forcing rigid processes onto creative work?

---

## Approach

I approached this as a maturity-building exercise — not a one-time fix, but a gradual layering of operational capability.

### Phase 1 — Visibility (Coordinator role, 2022-2023)

I started by creating a clear picture of the current state. I built centralized project tracking across the Wellbeing, SDA, and Brand Expression portfolios. I standardized how projects were documented and reported, making it possible for leadership to see the full landscape for the first time. I also established a SharePoint platform as the single source of truth for design documentation, replacing the patchwork of local files and personal drives.

### Phase 2 — Process design (Design Operations, 2024-2025)

With visibility in place, I shifted to designing workflows that actually fit how global teams work. I mapped existing processes, identified bottlenecks, and introduced lightweight frameworks for project intake, resource requests, and cross-team handoffs. The goal was always to remove friction rather than add bureaucracy. I facilitated workshops with design leads across regions to co-create these workflows — ensuring buy-in and practical fit.

### Phase 3 — Strategic operations (Senior Design Operations, 2025-present)

In my current role, I focus on the strategic layer. I work closely with design leadership to align operational priorities with business objectives, manage resource allocation across portfolios, and continuously improve the operational systems we built. I also lead initiatives around design awards coordination and brand research — activities that raise the visibility and influence of the design function within the broader organization.

---

## My Role

I owned the design operations function end to end across its evolution — from building the initial project tracking systems, to designing the workflows, to leading the operational strategy. I was the single point of accountability for ensuring design teams had the structure and support they needed to deliver effectively. This meant working across every level: hands-on with project data and tools, collaborative with design leads and managers, and strategic with senior leadership.

---

## Impact

- Established a centralized operations function where none previously existed, serving as the operational backbone for a 30-person design team managing 50+ concurrent projects
- Reduced coordination overhead for design leads by consolidating project tracking and documentation into a single platform
- Improved cross-team alignment through standardized workflows and regular operational cadences
- Increased project visibility for leadership, enabling more informed decision-making on priorities and resource allocation
- Progressed the DesignOps maturity from ad hoc coordination to a structured, strategic function over a three-year period

---

## Key Takeaway

Building a DesignOps function isn't about imposing process on creative teams — it's about removing the operational friction that prevents them from doing their best work. The most effective approach is to start with visibility, earn trust through quick wins, and gradually layer in structure that teams actually want to use. The role of DesignOps is to make the organization around design work as well as the design itself.
