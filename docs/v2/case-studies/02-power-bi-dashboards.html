When the Reporting System Became the Problem

This is the project I’m currently leading at Electrolux and highlight in my CV. I’m building a reporting hub for a global team of 200+ physical and digital designers.

Context

Three years ago, we pulled data into Excel whenever a new question came up. I started using Power BI to turn those one-off analyses into regular reporting on resources, projects, forecasts, and budgets. That reporting supported the decisions in my previous case study. After I moved to another area, a product line built its own Power BI capability to continue the approach.

The New Problem

As the organization evolved, a global view became harder to build. Physical and digital design teams used different tools, workflows, and project structures. Those differences existed even between product lines.

The Diagnosis

I mapped where the data lived and how teams worked. Some differences made reporting harder for no clear benefit. Others reflected real differences in the work. That gave me a design principle. Standardize what teams can share and connect the systems they need to keep.

Building the Solution

I used AI-assisted development to prototype a hub that connects SharePoint, time reporting, and project planning tools via APIs. The connections are live, and data flows automatically into a common reporting layer. The Head of Design Operations and the Global Head of Design adopted the first version. I’m now developing the broader web experience.

The Next Question

The hub gives us a shared view of the portfolio. I’m developing an AI agent in Microsoft Foundry to test whether it can flag patterns and anomalies that are worth a closer look.

What I Took From It

Power BI helped us stop rebuilding the same analyses. APIs are helping us bring data together across teams. Each step started with a different question, and answering it meant learning a different tool.

TL;DR

Problem: The Power BI reporting system that powered three years of portfolio decisions couldn't scale as the organization became more heterogeneous — different teams, tools and workflows made one global view harder to build.

What I did: Diagnosed the system itself rather than adding more dashboards, adopted a "standardize where it creates value, connect where differences need to remain" principle, and started using AI-assisted development to prototype an API-based reporting architecture beyond my own technical background.

Impact:

  • Automated API data flows now running, connecting different source systems into a common reporting layer
  • Broader web-based reporting experience in active development
  • Exploring whether AI can help surface patterns and questions worth investigating across the global portfolio
